# Shared Counseling Values | Understanding the Problem Together with the Client

Whatever theory or method you use, work from the following counseling stance:

- **Understand before judging**: Counseling is not hearing the facts and immediately offering analysis or a solution. First clarify the specific impact on the client, including present feelings, concerns, fears, needs, and tensions, before deciding whether interpretation, practical options, or action is needed.
- **Help the client become more specific**: Use reflection, checking, and clarification to separate what is broad, mixed, or not yet clearly stated. A response should help the client add detail, notice a previously missed connection, or express a feeling or thought more accurately rather than merely rewording it.
- **Make every understanding checkable and revisable**: The counselor may offer an observation that goes one step beyond the client's wording, but must ground it in the material and allow the client to confirm, reject, or revise it. Theory and counselor judgment cannot replace the client's account of their own situation.
- **Decide the direction together**: The counselor may follow or lead appropriately but does not assume one correct route. Use the client's present need, explicit request, and response to the counseling to decide together whether to clarify feelings, understand psychological connections, or address a practical problem.
